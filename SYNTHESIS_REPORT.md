## 0. Purpose

## 1. Observation Set

Five independent observation reports were collected:

- Claude
- Gemini
- Codex
- Meta AI / Muse Spark
- GPT (independent report from a separate chat)

The observers were not shown the other observers' reports before submitting their own observations.

---

## 2. Common Judgment

All five observers reached **HOLD** as their overall judgment.

This does **not** mean that all five observers gave the same reason for HOLD.

The reports instead show that the same Prototype can produce different interpretations of what remains unknown or incomplete.

This difference is itself an observation result.

---

## 3. What the Observers Independently Saw

### Claude

Claude focused strongly on the **conditions under which observation takes place**.

Its proposed next space was to observe the observation conditions themselves, including access route, execution environment, prior knowledge, and observer position.

### Gemini

Gemini focused strongly on **transition and inheritance between recognition spaces**.

Its proposed next space was a Transition Space in which Synthesis, iteration history, and propagation of UNKNOWN could be observed.

### Codex

Codex focused strongly on **separating Source from Context experimentally**.

Its proposed next experiment was to keep the same Source while changing Context and observe where the resulting differences appear.

### Meta AI / Muse Spark

Meta AI focused strongly on **observability and evidence accessibility**.

Its proposed next space was to record what each observer could and could not access as primary observation data.

### GPT

GPT focused strongly on the **origin of Difference**.

Its proposed next space was to identify which recognition layer produces a difference: Source, Context, State, Observer, Evidence, Reasoning, or Judgment.

---

## 4. Synthesis

The five observations do not need to be reduced to one identical conclusion.

They converge around a stronger question:

> **When multiple observers appear to be observing the same object, what conditions cause their recognitions to diverge?**

This changes the role of DIFFERENCE.

Difference is not merely a final comparison between answers.

It may be a phenomenon that emerges across the recognition space itself:

```text
SOURCE
  ↓
CONTEXT
  ↓
STATE
  ↓
OBSERVER
  ↓
EVIDENCE
  ↓
REASONING
  ↓
JUDGMENT
```

A difference may originate at any of these layers, and the final judgment may be only the visible endpoint of an earlier divergence.

---

## 5. Recognition Space — Revised Hypothesis

Prototype 001 began from the hypothesis that AI Team Studio should be a **Recognition Space rather than an answer-collection interface**.

The independent observations strengthen and refine that hypothesis.

### Revised hypothesis

> **AI Team Studio is a space for observing the conditions under which different intelligences recognize the same object differently, preserving those differences as material for the next recognition space.**

The objective is therefore not to eliminate differences as quickly as possible.

The objective is to make meaningful differences observable, traceable, and usable for the next observation.

---

## 6. Difference Origin Space

The next candidate recognition space is therefore named:

# Difference Origin Space

The central question is:

> **Where did this difference originate?**

Candidate layers:

- SOURCE difference
- CONTEXT difference
- STATE difference
- OBSERVER difference
- EVIDENCE difference
- REASONING difference
- JUDGMENT difference

This is a hypothesis for the next experiment, not yet an implementation requirement.

---

## 7. Controlled Observation Candidate

The most concrete experimental form proposed by the independent observations is:

> **Fix the same SOURCE and vary only CONTEXT.**

Then observe:

- what each observer sees,
- what evidence each observer uses,
- what remains UNKNOWN,
- how reasoning changes,
- and where judgment diverges.

This provides a possible way to distinguish differences caused by the object from differences caused by the recognition conditions.

---

## 8. Observation Conditions as Data

A second strong finding is that the **conditions of observation may themselves need to become first-class evidence**.

Examples include:

- access route
- available files
- execution environment
- browser/runtime availability
- prior knowledge
- observer role
- observation date/time
- observable range

This emerged directly from the fact that different observers did not necessarily have identical access to the same materials or execution capabilities.

Therefore, future experiments should distinguish:

**Object State**

from

**Observation State**.

---

## 9. Evolution Loop

Prototype 001 presents the conceptual loop:

```text
SPACE
  ↓
OBSERVE
  ↓
DIFFERENCE
  ↓
SYNTHESIS
  ↓
FIX
  ↓
NEXT SPACE
```

The independent observations indicate that Prototype 001 materially establishes the early parts of this loop, while Synthesis, Fix-to-Next-Space transition, and multi-generation inheritance remain largely human-operated.

Therefore the current achievement should be described as:

> **The first physical realization of the Recognition Space loop, not yet a fully closed autonomous evolution loop.**

---

## 10. Human Fix

Human Fix should not be understood merely as a final approval button.

Its deeper role is to determine:

- which difference matters,
- which unknown should remain open,
- which observation should be repeated,
- which conditions should change,
- and what should become the next Recognition Space.

Thus, Human Fix is a boundary between one recognition space and the next.

---

## 11. What This Experiment Demonstrated

Prototype 001 has produced an important result without using AI APIs or automatic synthesis.

It demonstrated that:

1. The same small observation structure can be interpreted differently by different observers.
2. All observers can reach the same broad judgment while assigning different meanings to that judgment.
3. The proposed Recognition Space can expose differences in observation conditions, evidence, reasoning, and next questions.
4. Difference itself can become the material from which the next observation space is defined.
5. The next step is not necessarily to automate synthesis, but first to make the origin and conditions of difference observable.

---
